## Disease and Normal Gene Function

Oculocutaneous Albinism Type 1 (OCA1) is a genetic condition characterized by reduced or absent production of melanin, the pigment responsible for the coloration of the skin, hair, and eyes. OCA1 is associated with variants in the TYR gene, which encodes the enzyme tyrosinase.

The TYR gene is located on chromosome 11 at 11q14.3 and normally encodes tyrosinase, a copper-containing enzyme involved in melanin production. Tyrosinase plays an essential role in melanogenesis and is associated with the melanosome, where melanin is synthesized. Changes that reduce or eliminate normal tyrosinase activity can disrupt melanin production and contribute to the hypopigmentation characteristic of OCA1.
